\section{Data compilation}

We compiled repeated forest inventories from multiple observational plot networks (Tab. \ref{tab:datasets}) and harmonised them into a consistent dataset containing plot-level tree density ($N$), quadratic mean diameter (QMD), geographic coordinates, and census year (Sec. \ref{sec:harmonisation}). These variables were either obtained directly or calculated from the source data, depending on the variables available and their level of aggregation (Sec. \ref{sec:constituentdatasets}). An overview of the global spatial coverage and record lengths are given in Sec. \ref{sec:datacoverage}

\subsection{Harmonisation of plot-level variables}
\label{sec:harmonisation}

The observational unit was a plot in a given calendar year. Depending on the source, a plot represented an inventory sample plot, a permanent monitoring plot, or a subplot within a larger mapped forest plot. The harmonised variables were stem density ($N$, stems ha$^{-1}$), quadratic mean diameter (QMD, cm), longitude and latitude, and census year. Basal area (BA, m$^2$ ha$^{-1}$) was retained as an intermediate variable. Dataset-specific identifiers are given below to link the descriptions to Table~\ref{tab:datasets}.

For datasets supplied as individual tree or stem records, we applied the source-specific status and measurement filters described below and aggregated the retained records by plot and year. For a plot of area $A$ ha containing $n$ retained records with diameters $d_i$ in centimetres, density and basal area were calculated as
\begin{equation}
N = \frac{n}{A}, \qquad
\mathrm{BA} = \frac{\pi}{4A}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{d_i}{100}\right)^2.
\label{eq:ba}
\end{equation}
Thus, the count $n$ is distinguished from the area-normalised density $N$. Where the source contained separate stem records, these records contributed separately to density. The corresponding QMD was
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2}.
\label{eq:qmd}
\end{equation}
For stand-level summaries, the supplied density and basal area were retained, with area normalisation where required. QMD was then calculated consistently from these quantities as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = 100\sqrt{\frac{4\,\mathrm{BA}}{\pi N}} \simeq \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{BA}}{0.00007854\,N}},
\label{eq:qmd2}
\end{equation}
where the factor of 100 converts metres to centimetres. The Norwegian inventory was an exception: its supplied QMD was retained after conversion to centimetres. The United States inventory used tree-specific expansion factors when calculating density and basal area.

For tree-level aggregation, density counted all retained records, whereas missing individual basal areas were omitted from basal-area sums. Consequently, the dataset-specific treatment of missing diameters determined whether the two quantities were based on the same set of measured stems.

The aggregation functions excluded observations with non-positive density values. Observations were ordered by plot and year to calculate intervals between successive measurements and the number of available observations. Source census identifiers were retained or regenerated from distinct years within each plot. There was no single minimum individual-tree diameter imposed by the compilation script across all sources. Source sampling thresholds and the explicit filters described below determined which records contributed to each summary. The subsequent requirement of QMD $\geq 10$ cm was a plot-level analysis filter, not an individual-tree diameter threshold.

Geographic coordinates were retained from source records or joined from plot metadata. Projected coordinates were transformed to WGS84 longitude and latitude using the source-specific coordinate reference systems described below. For several large mapped plots, all subplots were assigned the same site-level geographic coordinates. Local tree coordinates were used to identify or construct subplots. Biomes were assigned by overlaying coordinates on the WWF terrestrial ecoregion polygons \citep{olson01wwf}. Records without a biome assignment or outside the retained forest category were removed. Tundra biome assignments were recoded as boreal forest/taiga, and Mudumalai received the manual biome assignment described below. Geographic coordinates were also used to attach environmental covariates.

Management indicators and available information on forest-use history were harmonised as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:managementharmonisation}. The dataset-specific measurement exclusions below were applied during compilation, before the common analysis filters for management, repeated observations, and disturbance. Barro Colorado Island was processed in the raw-data script but was excluded from the combined dataset because management history could not be resolved sufficiently. It is therefore not included among the constituent datasets described below.

\subsection{Harmonisation of management information}
\label{sec:managementharmonisation}

We distinguished management during the observation period from forest-use history before the first census. The variable \texttt{management} was the indicator used for the former and we retained only plots that were not affected by management between censi (\texttt{management == 0}). Depending on the dataset, this value was retained from a supplied indicator, inferred from the status as forest reserve (for forest reserves, management was taken to be absent by definition), inferred from intervention-history information, or assigned to the supplied dataset as a whole.

Earlier forest-use history was represented by \texttt{management\_cat}. Category 1 denoted recorded history, for which an elapsed time or last-intervention date was available or an explicit interval was assigned from supplementary information. Category 2 denoted forests regarded as pristine, primary, old-growth, protected, or minimally used at the first census, generally without a quantified interval since intervention. Category 3 denoted unrecorded history or possible substantial previous use. These categories describe the information and classification used in the analysis. Forest reserve designation alone did not imply category 2: different forest reserve datasets were assigned to categories 1, 2, or 3 according to the treatment described below.

The variable \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} was intended to record the number of years between the last known management intervention and the first census. Where dates were available, it was calculated as the first measurement year minus the year of the last intervention. Other datasets received fixed intervals, obtained from data contributors. Missing intervals were stored as missing values, not as zero years since management. For tree-level datasets, the aggregation averaged the non-missing interval values across retained records within each plot-year. The value 999 was used as a numerical marker for a very long absence of intervention or a history interpreted as never managed, rather than a measured 999-year interval. The special Swiss NFI calculation is described separately below.

The ``Filter'' column in Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarises the reported selection criteria or management descriptions of the contributing sources. These descriptions were generally translated into harmonised variables. ``No management intervention observed during monitoring'' and ``Forest reserves'' led to assignment of \texttt{management = 0}. The identification of ``forest reserves'' was either based on a datast-specific code, definition by the data contributor, or a dataset-level assignment. The individual descriptions below distinguish these cases.

% The subsequent common filtering in \texttt{02\_create\_filters.R} retained records with \texttt{management = 0}. The additional management-history sensitivity filters required either category 1 with an interval of at least 30 or 100 years, respectively, or category 2 irrespective of the interval. Category 3 and category 1 with a missing interval were excluded from these stricter subsets. The category-2-only subset used \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. Thus, an unknown interval could be retained in category 2, whereas a numerical marker of 999 in category 1 passed both elapsed-time thresholds but did not qualify for the category-2-only subset.

\subsubsection{Spanish National Forest Inventory}
The Spanish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_spain}) was supplied as species-level summaries for each plot and census. We standardised plot identifiers, census year, coordinates, and the density and basal-area variables, then summed density and basal area across species within each plot and year. QMD was calculated from the resulting stand totals using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Plot area was recorded as 0.20 ha. The supplied per-hectare summaries were not divided by this area again. Source coordinates in ETRS89 / UTM zone 30N (EPSG:25830) were transformed to WGS84. The supplied plots had no registered management intervention during monitoring, while earlier management history was recorded as unknown.

The supplied selection of plots without registered management during monitoring was encoded as \texttt{management = 0}. No additional intervention filter was evaluated in this block. Earlier use was treated as unrecorded: \texttt{management\_cat = 3} and the elapsed-time variable was missing. Consequently, these records could enter the common unmanaged-data selection, but not the stricter category-1/category-2 management-history subsets.

\subsubsection{Swedish National Forest Inventory}
For the Swedish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_sweeden}), we used the supplied stand-level density and basal area, together with plot identifiers, geographic coordinates, and year. Plot identifiers were converted to numeric codes and census indices were generated within each plot. QMD was derived from stand basal area and density using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. The separate tree/species table was reshaped across its three census-specific basal-area columns and joined to a species-code lookup to identify the dominant species by basal area. This supplementary tree table did not replace the supplied stand-level density and basal area in the QMD calculation. The source stand table already contained a management indicator (0 for unmanaged and 1 for managed), which was retained for the later selection of records with \texttt{management = 0}. All records were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 3}, with a missing elapsed-time value.

\subsubsection{United States Forest Inventory and Analysis}
For the United States inventory (\texttt{fia\_us}), plot, condition, and tree tables were combined across states. A persistent plot identifier was formed from survey unit, state, county, and plot codes. Trees with live status (\texttt{STATUSCD = 1}) were aggregated by plot and inventory year. Density was calculated by summing the tree-specific expansion factor, \texttt{TPA\_UNADJ}, and dividing by 0.405 to convert stems per acre to stems per hectare. Basal area was calculated from squared diameters in inches, weighted by the same expansion factors, and multiplied by $0.00064516/0.405$ to obtain m$^2$ ha$^{-1}$. QMD was calculated from these expanded totals using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}, with the coefficient rounded to 0.0000785. Plot latitude and longitude were joined from the plot table, and forest reserve status was joined from the condition table for subsequent selection. Records with the placeholder inventory year 9999, non-positive density, or non-positive QMD were excluded.

The ``Forest reserves'' criterion was operationalised using \texttt{RESERVCD} from the condition table. The code retained the first condition-table record for each plot and assigned \texttt{management = RESERVCD - 1}; the later zero-value filter therefore selected plots whose retained reserve code was 1. This was a plot-level reserve-status proxy, not a census-by-census check of intervention history. All records were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 2}, and the elapsed-time variable was missing.

\subsubsection{Swiss National Forest Inventory}
For the Swiss inventory (\texttt{nfi\_switzerland}), we joined plot-census summaries to the plot metadata containing geographic coordinates. Calendar year was extracted from census dates. The source variables \texttt{NPH} and \texttt{BASAL\_AREA\_HA} provided density and basal area, respectively, and QMD was calculated using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Tree records and a species lookup were used separately to attach a dominant-species classification. The reported time since the last silvicultural intervention was retained and processed as management-history information for later filtering.

Management was inferred from \texttt{LETZTENU}, the reported years since the last silvicultural intervention at each census. Within each plot, the code first assigned an elapsed-time value of 999 if any census had the special code 999, including when other values were missing. Otherwise, any missing value led to a missing interval. For complete records, the interval was set to the sum of \texttt{LETZTENU} values if they increased strictly in the input order, and to missing otherwise. A non-missing result produced \texttt{management = 0}. A missing result produced \texttt{management = 1}. All plots were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1}.

\subsubsection{Swiss Experimental Forest Management plots}
For the Swiss Experimental Forest Management dataset (\texttt{efm\_swi}), we selected the stand-summary data, labelled in original files as ``All species combined''. These were joined to plot metadata, plot areas, geographic locations, and records of the last management intervention. Density and basal area were taken from the supplied all-species totals per hectare. Although a source quadratic-diameter variable was also available, the harmonised QMD was recalculated from density and basal area using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Records with zero or missing density were removed, census years were taken from the measurement-year field, and census indices were generated within each plot.

A separate intervention-history table provided the first measurement year and the year of the last intervention. Their difference was stored in \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} and joined by plot identifier. All records were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1} and \texttt{management = 0}. Thus, the table's within-monitoring criterion was represented by the supplied selection and a dataset-level assignment, while the elapsed interval was available for the later 30- and 100-year sensitivity filters.

\subsubsection{Greek forest plots}
For the Greek forest plots (\texttt{fep\_gre\_stand}), we used the supplied stand-level density, basal area per hectare, plot area, coordinates, and census year. \subsubsection{INRAE LESSEM plots}
For the French plots (\texttt{inrae\_lessem}), tree measurements were joined to plot metadata and species and status lookups. All aggregated records were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The tree-aggregation function expected \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} to be present in the joined input and averaged its non-missing values within each plot-year.

\subsubsection{Romanian reserve plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Romanian reserve dataset (\texttt{unitbv}), diameter, status, and biomass columns for 2003, 2013, and 2023 were reshaped into one record per tree and year. \subsubsection{Bavarian Institute of Forestry plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Bavarian Institute of Forestry dataset (\texttt{lwf}), unique plot locations and areas were extracted from the stand metadata and joined to the tree records. We retained living trees, used the source census field as measurement year, and calculated individual basal areas from diameters. \subsubsection{NPV-BW reserve plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the NPV-BW dataset (\texttt{nbw}), we retained tree records classified as alive and used the supplied plot areas to express stem counts and basal areas per hectare. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameters and QMD was derived from the plot-level totals using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. The source census field supplied the measurement year. To obtain a consistent plot location, latitude and longitude were averaged across the retained records belonging to each plot before aggregation.

The elapsed-time variable was calculated for each plot as the earliest observed census year minus \texttt{year\_last\_management}, then averaged across retained tree records during plot-year aggregation. All plots were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1} and \texttt{management = 0}.

\subsubsection{Swiss Natural Forest Reserves}
For the Swiss Natural Forest Reserves (\texttt{nfr\_swi}), we selected the stand-summary rows labelled ``All species combined'' and joined plot areas, reserve-level geographic coordinates, and management-history records. Measurement year was retained from the source, and within-plot census indices were generated from distinct years. The reserve summaries were joined to the last-intervention table, and the elapsed-time variable was calculated as \texttt{first\_measurement} minus \texttt{year\_last\_intervention}. \subsubsection{RAINFOR Amazon Forest Inventory Network}

\section{Negative density-size relationship analysis}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

% Prior to analysing \textit{trends} in the NDSR, we investigated whether a pattern of a NDSR emerges across plots and successivee inventories, and whether inidividual plots follow the NDSR, that is, a linear trajectory in a $\log N$--$\log \text{QMD}$-plot. Across all four major forest biomes, we find a clear expression of the NDSR and a median $\log N$--$\log \text{QMD}$ scaling slope of -1.25, -0.91, -1.1, -1.39 for Temperate, Mediterranean, Boreal, and Tropical/Subtropical forests, respectively. Repeated inventories from individual plots converge toward and then track the general NDSR, albeit with substantial plot-specific vertical offsets, likely driven by variation in growth conditions and species composition (Fig. \ref{fig:stl_longplots}). This supports the applicability of the NDSR framework for analysing mixed, age-structured forests, with plot-specific offsets accounted for via random effects and temporal changes in the vertical offset across all plots via fixed effects in linear quantile mixed models (LQMMs).
