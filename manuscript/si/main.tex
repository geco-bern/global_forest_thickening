\section{Data compilation}

We compiled repeated forest inventories from multiple observational plot networks (Tab. \ref{tab:datasets}) and harmonised them into a consistent dataset containing plot-level tree density ($N$), quadratic mean diameter (QMD), geographic coordinates, and census year (Sec. \ref{sec:harmonisation}). These variables were either obtained directly or calculated from the source data, depending on the variables available and their level of aggregation (Sec. \ref{sec:constituentdatasets}). An overview of the global spatial coverage and record lengths are given in Sec. \ref{sec:datacoverage}

\subsection{Harmonisation of plot-level variables}
\label{sec:harmonisation}

The observational unit was a plot in a given calendar year. Depending on the source, a plot represented an inventory sample plot, a permanent monitoring plot, or a subplot within a larger mapped forest plot. The harmonised variables were stem density ($N$, stems ha$^{-1}$), quadratic mean diameter (QMD, cm), longitude and latitude, and census year. Basal area (BA, m$^2$ ha$^{-1}$) was retained as an intermediate variable. Dataset-specific identifiers are given below to link the descriptions to Table~\ref{tab:datasets}.

For datasets supplied as individual tree or stem records, we applied the source-specific status and measurement filters described below and aggregated the retained records by plot and year. For a plot of area $A$ ha containing $n$ retained records with individual basal areas $b_i$ in m$^2$, density and basal area were calculated as
\begin{equation}
N = \frac{n}{A}, \qquad
\mathrm{BA} = \frac{1}{A}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_i.
\label{eq:ba}
\end{equation}
Thus, the count $n$ is distinguished from the area-normalised density $N$. Where the source contained separate stem records, these records contributed separately to density. Supplied individual basal areas were retained; a missing value was filled from that tree's diameter at breast height (DBH), $d_i$ in centimetres, as $b_i = \pi(d_i/200)^2$. Where all basal areas were derived from complete diameter measurements, the corresponding QMD was equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2}.
\label{eq:qmd}
\end{equation}
For stand-level summaries, the supplied density and basal area were retained, with area normalisation where required. Species-level density and basal area were summed within each plot and year. For every constituent dataset, QMD was calculated from the resulting density and basal area using a shared conversion function:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = 100\sqrt{\frac{4\,\mathrm{BA}}{\pi N}},
\label{eq:qmd2}
\end{equation}
where the factor of 100 converts metres to centimetres. The function used R's value of $\pi$, avoiding a rounded conversion coefficient. The Norwegian inventory was an exception: its provider-supplied QMD was retained after conversion from millimetres to centimetres, without recalculation from basal area and density. The United States inventory used tree-specific expansion factors when calculating density and basal area. The raw compilation retained density and QMD on their original scales, without creating separate \texttt{logDensity} or \texttt{logQMD} columns.

Missing measurements were retained during tree-level aggregation once a record met the source-specific status, location, and census criteria. A tree with missing DBH still contributed to density. If its basal area was supplied, that value contributed to the basal-area sum; otherwise, basal area was filled only from the DBH of the same tree. If both measurements were missing, individual basal area remained missing and the entire plot--year basal-area sum, and hence QMD, was set to missing. Basal-area sums therefore did not omit unmeasured trees or treat their basal area as zero. A missing diameter was not reconstructed from basal area or borrowed from another tree. Where an arithmetic mean DBH was retained as an auxiliary variable, it was calculated from available diameters and remained missing if none were available; this mean was distinct from QMD and was not used to fill missing stand basal area.

For species-level aggregation, a missing density or basal-area contribution made the corresponding stand total missing (\texttt{sum(..., na.rm = FALSE)}). A species mean DBH could not reconstruct its missing basal-area total. Likewise, missing FIA expansion factors or diameter contributions were propagated through the weighted sums. Density was not imputed from basal area or DBH. Missing density was retained by the species-aggregation and FIA filters, which excluded only known non-positive values. The stand-summary helpers, including the helper that joins supplementary tree information to stand summaries, still required positive, non-missing density; thus missing-density retention was not universal across sources. Missing QMD was retained at compilation where the other eligibility criteria were met, including by the explicit FIA and dataset-specific QMD filters. Retention at this stage did not imply that an incomplete observation could enter a subsequent model fit.

Observations were ordered by plot and year to calculate intervals between successive measurements and the number of available observations. Basal-area increments were missing when either endpoint basal area was missing. Source census identifiers were retained or regenerated from distinct years within each plot. There was no single minimum individual-tree diameter imposed by the compilation script across all sources. Source sampling thresholds and the explicit filters described below determined which records contributed to each summary. The subsequent requirement of QMD $\geq 10$ cm was a plot-level analysis filter, not an individual-tree diameter threshold.

Geographic coordinates were retained from source records or joined from plot metadata. Projected coordinates were transformed to WGS84 longitude and latitude using the source-specific coordinate reference systems described below. For several large mapped plots, all subplots were assigned the same site-level geographic coordinates. Local tree coordinates were used to identify or construct subplots. Biome assignment and environmental extraction were performed jointly for all constituent datasets after combining the stand records (Sec.~\ref{sec:environmentalextraction}).

Management indicators and available information on forest-use history were harmonised as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:managementharmonisation}. The dataset-specific measurement exclusions below were applied during compilation, before the common analysis filters for management, repeated observations, and disturbance. Barro Colorado Island was processed in the raw-data script but was excluded from the combined dataset because management history could not be resolved sufficiently. It is therefore not included among the constituent datasets described below.

\subsection{Harmonisation of management information}
\label{sec:managementharmonisation}

For all analyses, we retained only plots classified as having experienced no management between the first and last census (\texttt{management = 0}). This common requirement was applied to every constituent dataset, independently of forest-use history before the first census. The latter was described separately by \texttt{management\_cat} and \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. Depending on the dataset, this value was retained from a supplied indicator, inferred from intervention-history information or the contributor's description of a reserve dataset, or assigned to the supplied dataset as a whole. For the Swedish and United States inventories, explicit plot-history checks were applied during raw-data processing, excluding an entire plot when the required evidence of no management during monitoring was absent. Reserve status alone was insufficient for the United States inventory.

Earlier forest-use history was represented by \texttt{management\_cat}. Category 1 denoted recorded history, using an intervention date, a reported elapsed interval, stand age as a proxy, or an explicit interval assigned from supplementary information. A category-1 assignment did not always guarantee a non-missing interval, as described for Sweden and Switzerland below. Category 2 denoted forests regarded as pristine, primary, old-growth, protected, or minimally used at the first census, generally without a quantified interval since intervention. Category 3 denoted unrecorded history or possible substantial previous use. These categories describe the source information and classification used in the analysis; category 2 did not independently establish that a stand had never been affected by people. Forest reserve designation alone did not imply category 2: different forest reserve datasets were assigned to categories 1, 2, or 3 according to the treatment described below.

The variable \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} represented the interval before the first census. Where intervention dates were available, it was calculated as the first measurement year minus the year of the last intervention. The Swedish inventory used stand age at the first census, and the United States inventory used it when a dated prior treatment was unavailable. Stand age was a proxy for elapsed time, not a direct measurement of the time since the last harvest. Other datasets received one fixed interval for all plots: 64 years for Norway, 69 years for the Czech plots, 70 years for the Bulgarian plots, and 53 years for SCBI. These values were fixed compilation assignments rather than calculations from plot-specific dates. The processing notes did not consistently document their attribution to a provider; they are therefore distinguished from explicitly documented provider information in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. The Swedish, Swiss, and United States baseline intervals were held constant across subsequent censuses. Missing intervals were stored as missing values, not as zero years since management. For tree-level datasets, aggregation averaged the non-missing interval values across retained records within each plot-year. In some source-specific assignments, 999 marked a very long absence of intervention or a history interpreted as never managed, rather than a measured 999-year interval; this convention was not applied to FIA stand-age codes. The Swiss inventory used the reported years since intervention at the first census, as described below.

The \textit{Management history} column in Table~\ref{tab:datasets} describes differences in history before the first census, rather than repeating the common absence-of-management criterion. It distinguishes a single assignment applied to all plots from intervals or categories determined separately from plot records. \textit{Provider description} or \textit{provider instruction} denotes an explicit source description or contributor instruction documented in the processing notes. \textit{Interpretation} denotes a classification assigned during processing, including reserve/old-growth interpretations where no plot-level intervention history was available; it does not imply documented provider confirmation. A category-2 assignment with no quantified interval was therefore distinct from category 3, for which prior history remained unclassified or unknown. Missing dates or ages were not replaced with a common interval unless a fixed assignment is explicitly stated.

Evidence for no management during monitoring also differed among sources. For many constituent datasets, the provider's selection or description of the network was encoded as \texttt{management = 0}. For Sweden, the supplied management indicator had to be known and zero at every census. For Switzerland, \texttt{MANAGEMENT\_INDICATOR} had to equal 1 (no treatment since the preceding inventory) at every census after the first available census; the baseline indicator concerned earlier history. For FIA, every observed condition had to be forest and reserved, with additional checks for treatment, new stumps, and artificial regeneration during monitoring. The common \texttt{management = 0} filter was then applied in \texttt{02\_create\_filters.R}; records with missing management status were excluded. Thus, a retained plot could have a known or unknown history of management before monitoring without violating the common criterion.

% The subsequent common filtering in \texttt{02\_create\_filters.R} retained records with \texttt{management = 0}. The additional management-history sensitivity filters required either category 1 with an interval of at least 30 or 100 years, respectively, or category 2 irrespective of the interval. Category 3 and category 1 with a missing interval were excluded from these stricter subsets. The category-2-only subset used \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. Thus, an unknown interval could be retained in category 2, whereas a numerical marker of 999 in category 1 passed both elapsed-time thresholds but did not qualify for the category-2-only subset.

\subsection{Environmental information at plot locations}
\label{sec:environmentalextraction}

Raw-data processing in \texttt{00\_raw\_data.R} prepared stand variables and WGS84 coordinates. A user-set \texttt{execution\_environment} selected the local or server archive root, with the same source files and processing steps in either environment. The constituent datasets were combined in \texttt{01\_clean\_data.R}, with harmonised dataset names incorporated into plot identifiers to distinguish plots from different sources, and saved to \path{data/inputs/df_all.rds}. Environmental fields were then extracted in \path{00_collect_global_drivers.R} once for each distinct valid longitude--latitude pair across the entire compilation and joined back to all corresponding plot--census records. Thus, repeated censuses and subplots sharing coordinates received the same environmental values. This step retained all input records; missing or invalid coordinates, or unavailable field values, resulted in missing environmental covariates. No leaf-area-index field was extracted.

Biomes were assigned by overlaying plot locations on WWF terrestrial ecoregion polygons \citep{olson01wwf}. Tundra assignments were recoded as boreal forest/taiga, and Mudumalai was assigned to Tropical and Subtropical Dry Broadleaf Forests. The selection of forest biomes, including the exclusion of unassigned records, was deferred to \texttt{02\_create\_filters.R}. The annual aridity-index raster was aggregated to $0.1^\circ$ resolution and its stored values rescaled by $10^{-4}$. Mean nitrogen deposition was calculated for 1970--2009 by averaging oxidised and reduced nitrogen deposition separately and summing their means \citep{lamarque_global_2011}. Mean annual temperature was obtained by averaging the 12 monthly WorldClim temperature climatologies for 1970--2000 \citep{fick_worldclim_2017}. Soil C:N ratio and organic carbon were extracted from WISE \citep{batjes_harmonized_2016}, using depth-weighted summaries of the upper three source layers. The extraction also included the phosphorus variable \texttt{PBR} from the upper three GSDE/Shangguan layers when those data were available; runs explicitly omitting this field retained missing phosphorus values.

\subsubsection{Spanish National Forest Inventory}
The Spanish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_spain}) was supplied as species-level summaries for each plot and census. We standardised plot identifiers, census year, coordinates, and the density and basal-area variables, then summed density and basal area across species within each plot and year. QMD was calculated from the resulting stand totals using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Plot area was recorded as 0.20 ha. The supplied per-hectare summaries were not divided by this area again. Source coordinates in ETRS89 / UTM zone 30N (EPSG:25830) were transformed to WGS84. The supplied plots had no registered management intervention during monitoring, while earlier management history was recorded as unknown.

The supplied selection of plots without registered management during monitoring was encoded as \texttt{management = 0}. No additional intervention filter was evaluated in this block. Earlier use was treated as unrecorded: \texttt{management\_cat = 3} and the elapsed-time variable was missing. Consequently, these records could enter the common unmanaged-data selection, but not the stricter category-1/category-2 management-history subsets.

\subsubsection{Swedish National Forest Inventory}
For the Swedish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_sweeden}), we used the supplied stand-level density and basal area, together with plot identifiers, geographic coordinates, and year. Plot identifiers were converted to numeric codes and census indices were generated in chronological order within each plot. QMD was derived from stand basal area and density using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. The separate tree/species table was reshaped across its three census-specific basal-area columns and joined to a species-code lookup to identify the dominant species by basal area. This supplementary tree table did not replace the supplied stand-level density and basal area in the QMD calculation.

The source stand table contained a management indicator (0 for unmanaged and 1 for managed). We retained a plot only if this indicator was recorded as 0 at every available census; a managed census or a missing indicator excluded the entire plot. All retained records were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 1} (recorded history). We used the supplied \texttt{stand\_age} at the earliest available census as a proxy for the time since management before monitoring began, assigning this value to \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. This baseline interval was held constant across all censuses of the plot. If stand age was missing at the first census, the interval remained missing; an age from a later census was not substituted, and the plot retained category 1. Stand age did not establish the date of the last harvest or qualify the plot as old-growth.

\subsubsection{United States Forest Inventory and Analysis}
For the United States inventory (\texttt{fia\_us}), we combined plot, condition, and individual-tree records across states. Stand density and basal area were calculated from live trees (\texttt{STATUSCD = 1}) using the supplied tree-specific expansion factors (\texttt{TPA\_UNADJ}) and diameters (\texttt{DIA}), converted to per-hectare units; QMD was derived from these totals using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. The expansion factors accounted for differences in sampling area among plot designs and tree diameter classes,\footnote{FIA database user guide, section 1.2.5: \url{https://research.fs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/wo-v9-4_Aug2025_UG_FIADB_database_description_NFI.pdf}.} so no common plot area was assumed. Missing tree contributions were propagated to the stand totals as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:harmonisation}.

We retained only plots whose conditions were consistently classified as forest (\texttt{COND\_STATUS\_CD = 1}) and reserved (\texttt{RESERVCD = 1}) throughout monitoring and for which treatment records supported the absence of management between the first and last census. Treatment codes (\texttt{TRTCD1--3}) and corresponding years (\texttt{TRTYR1--3}) identified interventions. Treatment during this period, undated treatment, newly recorded stumps (\texttt{STUMP\_CD\_PNWRS = Y}), or newly recorded artificial regeneration (\texttt{STDORGCD = 1}) excluded the entire plot. Required condition and treatment information had to be available. Treatments explicitly dated before monitoring, and stump or artificial-origin indications already present at baseline, did not by themselves exclude a plot. These criteria were evaluated across all conditions within each plot, consistently with the whole-plot aggregation of trees. All retained plots received \texttt{management = 0}.

History before monitoring was classified separately using \texttt{management\_cat}. Assignment to \texttt{management\_cat = 2} required all baseline conditions to carry the regional old-growth classification (\texttt{STND\_COND\_CD\_PNWRS = 7}) and indicate natural origin (\texttt{STDORGCD = 0}), no stumps (\texttt{STUMP\_CD\_PNWRS = N}), and no baseline treatment, with no dated prior treatment in the available history. Reserve status or high stand age alone was insufficient. The shortest condition-level interval was assigned to \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} and held constant across censuses. Remaining plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 3} (unknown history). For categories 2 and 3, \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} was missing. Thus, some plots received coordinates representative of their site group rather than a separately matched plot location. Plot area was retained from the source summary table.

The supplied stand-summary fields \texttt{number\_stems} and \texttt{total\_BA} were used as the density and basal-area inputs, respectively, without an additional division by plot area in this script. QMD was calculated from their ratio using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Decimal census dates were rounded to the nearest calendar year. Geographic coordinates were assembled from two ancillary coordinate tables.

Plots were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. Forest plots of the RAINFOR network have been described as old-growth \citep{phillips09sci}, ``lacking signs of recent anthropogenic impacts'' \citep{brienen_long-term_2015}, and ``structurally intact'' \citep{bennett23natcc}.

\section{Negative density-size relationship analysis}

% Prior to analysing \textit{trends} in the NDSR, we investigated whether a pattern of a NDSR emerges across plots and successivee inventories, and whether inidividual plots follow the NDSR, that is, a linear trajectory in a $\log N$--$\log \text{QMD}$-plot. Across all four major forest biomes, we find a clear expression of the NDSR and a median $\log N$--$\log \text{QMD}$ scaling slope of -1.25, -0.91, -1.1, -1.39 for Temperate, Mediterranean, Boreal, and Tropical/Subtropical forests, respectively. Repeated inventories from individual plots converge toward and then track the general NDSR, albeit with substantial plot-specific vertical offsets, likely driven by variation in growth conditions and species composition (Fig. \ref{fig:stl_longplots}). This supports the applicability of the NDSR framework for analysing mixed, age-structured forests, with plot-specific offsets accounted for via random effects and temporal changes in the vertical offset across all plots via fixed effects in linear quantile mixed models (LQMMs).
