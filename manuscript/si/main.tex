\section{Data compilation}

We compiled repeated forest inventories from multiple observational plot networks (referred to as `constituent datasets', Tab. \ref{tab:datasets}) and harmonised them into a consistent dataset (Sec. \ref{sec:harmonisation}). These variables were either obtained directly or calculated from the source data, depending on the variables available and their level of aggregation (Sec. \ref{sec:constituentdatasets}). An overview of the global spatial coverage and record lengths is given in Sec.~\ref{sec:datacoverage}. Patterns of the negative size-density relationship are shown for each constituent dataset in Figs. \ref{fig:dataset-logn-logqmd1} and \ref{fig:dataset-logn-logqmd2}.

\subsection{Harmonisation of plot-level variables}
\label{sec:harmonisation}

The observational unit was a plot in a given calendar year. Depending on the source, a plot represented an inventory sample plot, a permanent monitoring plot, or a subplot within a larger mapped forest plot. The harmonised variables were stem density ($N$, stems ha$^{-1}$), quadratic mean diameter (QMD, cm), longitude and latitude, and census year. Basal area (BA, m$^2$ ha$^{-1}$) was retained as an intermediate variable. Dataset-specific identifiers are given below to link the descriptions to Table~\ref{tab:datasets}.

For datasets supplied as individual tree or stem records, we applied the source-specific status and measurement filters described below and aggregated the retained records by plot and year. For a plot of area $A$ ha containing $n$ retained records with individual basal areas $b_i$ in m$^2$, density and basal area were calculated as
\begin{equation}
N = \frac{n}{A}, \qquad
\mathrm{BA} = \frac{1}{A}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_i.
\label{eq:ba}
\end{equation}
Thus, the count $n$ is distinguished from the area-normalised density $N$. Where the source contained separate stem records, these records contributed separately to density. Supplied individual basal areas were retained. A missing value was filled from that tree's diameter at breast height (DBH), $d_i$ in centimetres, as $b_i = \pi(d_i/200)^2$. Where all basal areas were derived from complete diameter measurements, the corresponding QMD was equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2}.
\label{eq:qmd}
\end{equation}
For stand-level summaries, the supplied density and basal area were retained, with area normalisation where required. Species-level density and basal area were summed within each plot and year. Except where a source QMD was explicitly retained, QMD was calculated from the resulting density and basal area using a shared conversion function:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{QMD} = 100\sqrt{\frac{4\,\mathrm{BA}}{\pi N}},
\label{eq:qmd2}
\end{equation}
where the factor of 100 converts metres to centimetres. The Norwegian inventory was an exception: its provider-supplied QMD was retained after conversion from millimetres to centimetres, without recalculation from basal area and density. The United States forest inventory (NFI) used tree-specific expansion factors when calculating density and basal area. Missing individual basal area was filled only from that tree's DBH. If neither was available, the plot--year basal-area sum and QMD remained missing. Species-level and FIA weighted sums likewise propagated missing contributions. Aggregation used calendar year, with source-specific treatment of censuses spanning several years as described below.

Geographic coordinates were retained from source records or joined from plot metadata. Projected coordinates were transformed to WGS84 longitude and latitude using the source-specific coordinate reference systems described below. For several large mapped plots, all subplots were assigned the same site-level geographic coordinates. Where a subplot classification of individual trees was not available, local tree coordinates were used to construct subplots. Biome assignment and environmental extraction were performed jointly for all constituent datasets after combining the stand records (Sec.~\ref{sec:environmentalextraction}).

Management information was harmonised as described below. The combined dataset comprised 31 constituent datasets. Barro Colorado Island, Palanan and the separate ForestPlots compilations were processed but excluded from the combined analyses (Sec.~\ref{sec:excludedcompilations}) due to insufficient information for data processing (ForestPlots) or apparent relationships of QMD and density that exhibited no signs of self-thinning across available subplot data upon visual inspection (Barro Colorado Island, Palanan).

\subsection{Harmonisation of management information}
\label{sec:managementharmonisation}

All analyses retained only observations classified as having no management during monitoring, i.e., between censi (\texttt{management = 0}). This classification came either from recorded intervention information, a provider-selected unmanaged data subset, or an assumption about the supplied network. These sources of evidence are distinguished in the dataset descriptions. Where intervention records were available, plot histories were evaluated across censuses.

History before monitoring was represented separately by \texttt{management\_cat}: category 1 denoted recorded history or an assigned elapsed interval between the year of last management and the year of the first available census. Category 2 denoted a primary, old-growth or minimally used forest plot. Category 3 denoted unknown prior history. Category 2 was based on source descriptions or information provided by data contributors. Category 1 could retain a missing interval where the dataset's classification was known but a plot-specific date or age was unavailable.

The variable \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} described elapsed years before the first census. It was obtained from a reported baseline interval, the difference between first-census and last-intervention years, or stand age at the first census as a proxy. Baseline values were held constant across subsequent censuses where plot histories were reconstructed. Fixed dataset-wide assignments remained for CZU (69 yr), IBERBAS (70 yr) and SCBI (53 yr). The Swiss NFI value 999 denoted a very long absence of intervention or an effectively never-managed history, not a measured 999-year interval.

Management-history sensitivity analyses retained category 2 together with category 1 having at least 30 or 100 yr before the first census. The strictest history subset retained category 2 or category 1 with an interval of at least 300 yr, thereby including the Swiss NFI marker 999. Category 3 and category 1 with missing intervals did not qualify for these history subsets. Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarises how prior history was assigned. The common \texttt{management = 0} requirement applies to every dataset.

\subsection{Environmental information at plot locations}
\label{sec:environmentalextraction}

WGS84 plot coordinates were used to extract values of environmental covariates from global raster datasets and to classify each forest plot into a biome based on global biome polygons. Repeated censuses and subplots sharing coordinates therefore received the same values. Missing coordinates or unavailable field values produced missing covariates. Biomes were assigned by overlaying plot locations on WWF Terrestrial Ecoregion polygons \citep{olson01wwf}. Tundra assignments were recoded as boreal forest/taiga, and Mudumalai was assigned to Tropical and Subtropical Dry Broadleaf Forests. For analysis, moist and dry tropical/subtropical broadleaf forests were combined, as were temperate broadleaf/mixed and conifer forests. Boreal and Mediterranean forests formed the other two major biomes. Environmental covariates were the aridity-index \citep{zomer22}, mean annual temperature from the WorldClim climatologies for 1970--2000 \citep{fick_worldclim_2017}, nitrogen deposition mean annual (1970--2009) nitrogen deposition \citep{lamarque_global_2011} (sum of oxidised and reduced reactive nitrogen species), soil C:N ratio and organic carbon from WISE \citep{batjes_harmonized_2016}, using depth-weighted summaries of the upper three source layers, and soil phosphorous availability in the upper three layers from \citet{shangguan14james}. The same datasets were used as predictors for global upscaling of the C sink related to forest negative size-density relationship changes. For that, original rasters were remapped to a common global grid of $0.1^\circ$ resolution.

\subsubsection{Spanish National Forest Inventory}
The Spanish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_spain}) supplied species-level density (\texttt{dens}) and basal area for each plot and census. These per-hectare quantities were summed across species to obtain stand totals and QMD. Coordinates \texttt{CX\_ETRS89} and \texttt{CY\_ETRS89} were transformed from ETRS89 / UTM zone 30N (EPSG:25830) to WGS84. The providers supplied plots without registered management during monitoring, encoded as \texttt{management = 0}. Earlier history was unrecorded: \texttt{management\_cat = 3} and \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} missing.

\subsubsection{Swedish National Forest Inventory}
For the Swedish inventory (\texttt{nfi\_sweeden}), stand density and \texttt{basal\_area} supplied the stand metrics; supplementary tree/species data identified the dominant species. We retained only plots whose supplied \texttt{management} indicator was known and zero at every census. Retained plots received \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The source \texttt{stand\_age} at the first available census was used as a proxy for \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}, held constant thereafter. Missing first-census age remained missing. Stand age did not establish a harvest date or an old-growth classification.

% \input{dataset_figures/nfi_sweeden.tex}

\subsubsection{United States Forest Inventory and Analysis}
For the United States inventory (\texttt{fia\_us}), live trees (\texttt{STATUSCD = 1}) were aggregated using diameters \texttt{DIA} and tree-specific expansion factors \texttt{TPA\_UNADJ}. Density and basal area were converted to per-hectare units and QMD was derived from their ratio. These expansion factors accounted for sampling-area differences among plot designs and diameter classes; no common plot area was assumed.

A plot qualified for \texttt{management = 0} only when all observed conditions were forest (\texttt{COND\_STATUS\_CD = 1}) and reserved (\texttt{RESERVCD = 1}), with condition records available for each census. Follow-up treatment codes \texttt{TRTCD1--3} and dates \texttt{TRTYR1--3}, newly recorded stumps (\texttt{STUMP\_CD\_PNWRS}) or newly recorded artificial regeneration (\texttt{STDORGCD}) could disqualify the plot. Explicitly dated pre-monitoring treatments and stump or artificial-origin indications already present at baseline did not alone exclude it.

Category 2 required all baseline conditions to indicate regional old-growth status (\texttt{STND\_COND\_CD\_PNWRS = 7}), natural origin (\texttt{STDORGCD = 0}), no stumps (\texttt{STUMP\_CD\_PNWRS = N}) and no treatment evidence. Otherwise, \texttt{management\_cat = 1} required a prior-treatment date or valid baseline \texttt{STDAGE} for every condition. Treatment dates took precedence over stand age, and the shortest condition-level interval defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. Remaining plots received category 3. Categories 2 and 3 had missing intervals. Reserve status or high age alone did not establish category 2.

% \input{dataset_figures/fia_us.tex}

\subsubsection{Swiss National Forest Inventory}
For the Swiss inventory (\texttt{nfi\_switzerland}), density \texttt{NPH} and basal area \texttt{BASAL\_AREA\_HA} supplied the stand metrics. Supplementary tree records identified the dominant species. We retained \texttt{management = 0} only where \texttt{MANAGEMENT\_INDICATOR = 1} (no treatment since the preceding inventory) at every follow-up census. The baseline indicator concerned earlier history and was not part of this interval check.

All retained plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The Forest Service's reported years since intervention, \texttt{LETZTENU}, at the first census defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}, held constant thereafter. The special value 999 was retained as a marker of very long absence of intervention, rather than a measured interval or an automatic category-2 assignment.

% \input{dataset_figures/nfi_switzerland.tex}

\subsubsection{Norwegian National Forest Inventory}
For the Norwegian inventory (\texttt{nfi\_norway}), we retained stand records with \texttt{class = F\_Forest} and used the supplied density, basal area, area and coordinates. Provider-supplied \texttt{qmd} was converted from millimetres to centimetres and retained , including missing values, without recalculation from basal area. The supplied forest records were assumed unmanaged during monitoring (\texttt{management = 0}). No intervention-history test was available, therefore: \texttt{management\_cat = 3} and \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} missing.

% \input{dataset_figures/nfi_norway.tex}

\subsubsection{Swiss Experimental Forest Management plots}
For the Swiss Experimental Forest Management plots (\texttt{efm\_swi}), the ``All species combined'' summaries supplied per-hectare density and basal area, with plot areas and coordinates from metadata. QMD was recalculated from these stand totals. The separate intervention table supplied \texttt{first\_measurement} and \texttt{year\_last\_intervention}. Their difference defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. All plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The supplied selection was treated as unmanaged during monitoring (\texttt{management = 0}), without an additional census-by-census intervention check.

% \input{dataset_figures/efm_swi.tex}

\subsubsection{Uholka-Shyrokyi Luh}
For Uholka-Shyrokyi Luh (\texttt{uholka}), records with \texttt{status\_2 = 1} identified living trees. DBH was the mean of \texttt{dbh\_1} and \texttt{dbh\_2}. Both measurements were required. Trees were aggregated to 0.25 ha plots, with missing basal areas treated as in Sec.~\ref{sec:harmonisation}. The provider description identified primary forest without past management. This was encoded as \texttt{management = 0}, \texttt{management\_cat = 2} and a missing \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}.

% \input{dataset_figures/uholka.tex}

\subsubsection{Greek forest plots}
For the Greek forest plots (\texttt{fep\_gre\_stand}), stand density \texttt{N\_ha} and basal area \texttt{Basal\_area\_m2\_ha.1} supplied the stand metrics. Living-tree records identified the dominant species. In \texttt{info\_management}, ``Control plot (no thinning)'' (including the source spelling ``Contol'') and numeric entries indicated no management, while thinning labels indicated treatment. A plot received \texttt{management = 0} only if every census indicated no management. Treated or unresolved histories were excluded. All retained plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The source \texttt{years\_since\_management} at the first census defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}, held constant thereafter, including missing baseline values.

% \input{dataset_figures/fep_gre_stand.tex}

\subsubsection{INRAE LESSEM plots}
For the French plots (\texttt{inrae\_lessem}), living-tree diameters were combined with plot areas \texttt{area\_ha} to derive per-hectare stand metrics. Plot metadata supplied \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} alongside \texttt{first\_year} and \texttt{date\_last\_harvest}. We retained the supplied interval rather than reinterpreting qualitative harvest dates such as ``1971 or before''. The supplied plots were treated as unmanaged during monitoring (\texttt{management = 0}) and assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1}.

% \input{dataset_figures/inrae_lessem.tex}

\subsubsection{Berchtesgaden National Park (EuFoRIa)}
For Berchtesgaden National Park (\texttt{bnp}), the stand summaries supplied density, \texttt{basal\_area}, dominant species and WGS84 coordinates. QMD was calculated from the per-hectare totals. Plots were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}, following data contributor information. Where available, \texttt{year\_last\_management} also supplied a plot-specific interval relative to the first census, retained as \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. This did not override the provider-based category-2 classification.

% \input{dataset_figures/bnp.tex}

\subsubsection{Czech University of Life Sciences Prague plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Czech plots (\texttt{czu}), stand-level density and \texttt{basal\_area\_m2\_ha} supplied the stand metrics. \texttt{plot\_size\_m2} supplied plot area. The source described a reserve established in 1955, with low-intensity salvage logging until 1990. Monitoring records were treated as unmanaged (\texttt{management = 0}). All plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 1} and a fixed \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs = 69}. This was a compilation assignment, not an interval calculated from plot-specific census and intervention dates.

% \input{dataset_figures/czu.tex}

\subsubsection{Baden-W\"urttemberg forest research plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Baden-W\"urttemberg plots (\texttt{fvabw}), species-level \texttt{stem\_count\_ha} and \texttt{basal\_area\_ha} were summed to stand totals. Coordinates \texttt{utm\_x} and \texttt{utm\_y} were transformed from ETRS89 / UTM zone 32N (EPSG:25832) to WGS84. The forest-reserve dataset was treated as unmanaged during monitoring (\texttt{management = 0}). All plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 3}, with \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} missing.

% \input{dataset_figures/fvabw.tex}

\subsubsection{Bulgarian biodiversity research plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Bulgarian plots (\texttt{iberbas}), we used the individual-tree records and supplied plot areas, coordinates, and measurement years. Records with a known zero diameter were excluded, while records with missing diameter were retained. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameters and summed by plot and year, propagating any remaining missing basal area. Density was calculated as the number of retained records divided by plot area, including records with missing diameter, and QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. No additional live/dead-status filter was applied.

The reserve dataset was assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. A fixed elapsed-time value of 70 years was assigned.

% \input{dataset_figures/iberbas.tex}

\subsubsection{Romanian reserve plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Romanian reserve dataset (\texttt{unitbv}), diameter, status, and biomass columns for 2003, 2013, and 2023 were reshaped into one record per tree and year, retaining missing diameter entries. Local $X$ and $Y$ coordinates were used to divide the 100 $\times$ 100 m mapped area into 20 $\times$ 20 m subplots, each assigned an area of 0.04 ha and a unique subplot identifier. Only records classified as alive were retained. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameters, then density, basal area, and QMD were calculated for each subplot and year. Geographic coordinates were carried forward from the source records.

The reserve/old-growth interpretation was encoded as \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. The original dataset was described as lacking management-history information, so the elapsed-time variable was set to missing for all subplots.

% \input{dataset_figures/unitbv.tex}

\subsubsection{Bavarian Institute of Forestry plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Bavarian Institute of Forestry dataset (\texttt{lwf}), unique plot locations and areas were extracted from the stand metadata and joined to the tree records. We retained living trees, used the source census field as measurement year, and calculated individual basal areas from diameters. Density and basal area were aggregated per hectare by plot and year, and QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Coordinates supplied in ETRS89 / UTM zone 32N (EPSG:25832) were transformed to WGS84.

The source forest-reserve designation was represented by assigning \texttt{management = 0}. Earlier use was classified as unrecorded: \texttt{management\_cat = 3}, with a missing elapsed-time value.

% \input{dataset_figures/lwf.tex}

\subsubsection{Bavarian Forest National Park, Germany (EuFoRIa)}
For NPV-BW (\texttt{nbw}), living-tree diameters and supplied \texttt{plot\_size} were used to derive stand metrics. Plot histories were assessed before selecting living trees, using \texttt{census} and \texttt{year\_last\_management}. Any recorded intervention between the first and last census years, inclusive, assigned \texttt{management = 1} to the whole plot. A wholly missing management year left status unknown. Retained unmanaged plots received \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. Where the latest recorded intervention preceded the first census, their difference defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}, held constant across censuses.

% \input{dataset_figures/nbw.tex}

\subsubsection{Swiss Natural Forest Reserves}
For the Swiss Natural Forest Reserves (\texttt{nfr\_swi}), we selected the stand-summary rows labelled ``All species combined'' and joined plot areas, reserve-level geographic coordinates, and management-history records. Density and basal area were taken from the supplied per-hectare totals, and QMD was recalculated using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Records with zero or missing density were excluded. Eight plots identified by the data provider as having disproportionately large basal-area and biomass increments were also excluded. Measurement year was retained from the source, and within-plot census indices were generated from distinct years. The reserve summaries were joined to the last-intervention table, and the elapsed-time variable was calculated as \texttt{first\_measurement} minus \texttt{year\_last\_intervention}. All records were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. The supplied reserve selection was treated as unmanaged during monitoring (\texttt{management = 0}) without an additional intervention-history test.

% \input{dataset_figures/nfr_swi.tex}

\subsubsection{Northwest German Forest Research Institute plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Northwest German Forest Research Institute plots (\texttt{nwfva}), living trees (\texttt{status = A}) were linked to stand metadata. Diameters \texttt{dbhmm} were aggregated using \texttt{plot\_size}. Coordinates were transformed from EPSG:31467 to WGS84. The supplied selection was treated as unmanaged (\texttt{management = 0}). The first census year minus \texttt{year\_last\_management} defined \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}. Plots with an available interval received \texttt{management\_cat = 1}. Missing intervals led to category 3.

% \input{dataset_figures/nwfva.tex}

\subsubsection{Technical University in Zvolen plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Slovak plots (\texttt{tuzvo}), plot coordinates and areas were extracted from the stand metadata and joined to individual-tree observations. We retained records classified as alive and used the inventory-year field as the census year. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameters. Stem counts and basal areas were aggregated within each plot and year and divided by plot area, after which QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Geographic coordinates were retained from the plot metadata. The source was treated as a reserve/old-growth dataset without a quantified management history. All records were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. 

% \input{dataset_figures/tuzvo.tex}

\subsubsection{University of Ljubljana plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the Slovenian plots (\texttt{ul}), tree records were joined to separate plot-area and geographic-coordinate tables using the plot identifier. Only records classified as alive were retained. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameters, and density and basal area were aggregated by plot and measurement year. QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}, with geographic coordinates retained from the coordinate table and census indices regenerated within each plot. The source description characterised these plots as old-growth forest without management. This was encoded as \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}.

% \input{dataset_figures/ul.tex}

\subsubsection{University of Turin plots (EuFoRIa)}
For the University of Turin plots (\texttt{unito}), local tree-coordinate fields \texttt{lon} and \texttt{lat} divided parent plots 9\_1, 9\_2 and 9\_3 into $50\times50$~m subplots (0.25 ha). The marginal group in each parent was excluded because it did not represent a regular full subplot. Living trees (\texttt{status = A}) supplied density and basal area. Parent coordinates \texttt{lon\_plot} and \texttt{lat\_plot} were transformed from WGS84 / UTM zone 32N (EPSG:32632) to WGS84 longitude and latitude. The reserve/old-growth interpretation was encoded as \texttt{management = 0}, \texttt{management\_cat = 2} and a missing \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}.

% \input{dataset_figures/unito.tex}

\subsubsection{Roztocze National Park (EuFoRIa)}
For Roztocze National Park (\texttt{urk}), the input already contained stand-level density, basal area, geographic coordinates, and measurement year. QMD was calculated from density and basal area using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}, and the supplied area was retained as plot area. No aggregation of individual trees was required. The reserve/old-growth interpretation was represented by \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. The processing did not calculate an elapsed interval from the supplied \texttt{year\_last\_management} field.

% \input{dataset_figures/urk.tex}

\subsubsection{Bia\l{}owie\.{z}a National Park (EuFoRIa)}
For Bia\l{}owie\.{z}a National Park (\texttt{wuls}), diameter and status columns for eight censuses were reshaped into one record per tree and census, retaining missing diameter entries. Records coded as absent (0) or dead (3) were removed. Individual basal areas were calculated from diameter values. Transect and plot identifiers were combined to distinguish plots, and the records were joined to census-year, plot-area, geographic-coordinate, and species tables. Density and basal area were calculated per hectare for each plot and year, and QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. The reserve/old-growth interpretation was represented by \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}.

\subsubsection{Luquillo (ForestGEO)}
For Luquillo (\texttt{luquillo}), census files were combined and the supplied quadrat (subplot) identifier defined the analysis plot, with an assigned area of 0.04 ha. We retained living records with positive or missing diameter and non-missing local coordinates and year. Census years were explicitly assigned as 1990, 1994, 2000, 2005, 2011, and 2016 for censuses 1--6. Individual basal areas were aggregated by quadrat and year, and density and QMD were calculated using Eqs.~\ref{eq:ba} and \ref{eq:qmd2}. All quadrats were assigned the site coordinates 65.8160$^\circ$ W, 18.3262$^\circ$ N. The dataset was assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. More info available at \url{https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/north-america/luquillo}.

\subsubsection{Smithsonian Conservation Biology Institute (ForestGEO)}
For the Smithsonian Conservation Biology Institute site in Virginia, USA (\texttt{scbi}), census files were combined after harmonising the data types of tree and stem tags. The supplied quadrats were treated as 0.04 ha plots. Only living records with positive or missing diameter and non-missing local coordinates and year were retained. Census years were assigned as 2008, 2013, and 2018 for censuses 1--3. Density and basal area were aggregated by quadrat and year and QMD was calculated using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. All quadrats were assigned the site coordinates 78.1454$^\circ$ W, 38.8935$^\circ$ N. All records were assigned \texttt{management = 0}, \texttt{management\_cat = 1}, and a fixed elapsed-time value of 53 years. More information is available at \url{https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/north-america/smithsonian-conservation-biology-institute}.

% \input{dataset_figures/scbi.tex}


\subsubsection{Smithsonian Environmental Research Center (ForestGEO)}
For the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center (\texttt{serc}), local coordinates \texttt{PX} and \texttt{PY} defined $40\times40$~m cells (0.16 ha) within the mapped 400 m domain. Living records with positive or missing \texttt{DBH} supplied the stand metrics. Measurement years derived from \texttt{Date} were harmonised to the most frequent year within each subplot and \texttt{Census}, with ties resolved to the earliest year. This prevented a single subplot census from being split into partial annual inventories. The current processing retained missing QMD but excluded known $\ln(\mathrm{QMD})\leq1$. The network was treated as unmanaged and old-growth (\texttt{management = 0}, \texttt{management\_cat = 2}), with a missing baseline interval. All cells shared the site coordinates 76.5594$^\circ$ W, 38.8891$^\circ$ N.

% \input{dataset_figures/serc.tex}

\subsubsection{Wytham Woods (ForestGEO)}
For Wytham Woods (\texttt{wytham}), diameter fields \texttt{dbh\_2008}, \texttt{dbh\_2010}, \texttt{dbh\_2016} and \texttt{dbh\_2021} supplied repeated tree measurements, converted from millimetres to centimetres and aggregated to the supplied 0.04 ha plots. Positive or missing diameters and dated observations were retained. No status-code filter was applied, so the absence of a 2008 status field did not remove that census. All plots shared the site coordinates 1.3379$^\circ$ W, 51.7743$^\circ$ N. The network was treated as unmanaged and old-growth (\texttt{management = 0}, \texttt{management\_cat = 2}), without a quantified \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs}.

% \input{dataset_figures/wytham.tex}

\subsubsection{Pasoh (ForestGEO)}
For Pasoh (\texttt{pasoh}), seven census tables were combined and joined to a species-name lookup. The supplied quadrat (subplot) identifiers defined 0.04 ha analysis plots. Living records with positive or missing diameter and a known year were retained. Density and basal area were aggregated by quadrat and calendar year, and QMD was calculated using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Observations with $\ln(N) \leq 7.5$ were subsequently excluded as potential errors. All quadrats were assigned the site coordinates 102.30816760$^\circ$ E, 2.97956222$^\circ$ N. The dataset was assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. More information is available at \url{https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/asia/pasoh}.

\subsubsection{Mudumalai (ForestGEO)}
For Mudumalai (\texttt{mudumalai}), the source contained stand-level counts and basal-area totals. The number of individuals and total basal area were each divided by the supplied plot area to obtain density and basal area per hectare. QMD was derived using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Plot identifiers, geographic coordinates, measurement years, and census identifiers were retained from the source. Following the spatial biome assignment, the retained observations were explicitly assigned to Tropical and Subtropical Dry Broadleaf Forests. All stand records were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. More information is available at \url{https://forestgeo.si.edu/sites/asia/mudumalai}.

\subsubsection{Australian forest plots}
For the Australian compilation (\texttt{aus\_plots}), \texttt{TreesPerHectareAHC1\_2} and \texttt{BasalAreaAHC1\_2\_m2perha} supplied stand density and basal area. QMD was recalculated from these totals. Plot area, dominant species and coordinates were retained from the source summaries.

Management history was reconstructed separately for each plot from \texttt{year\_last\_management}, using the latest reported intervention and the calendar year of its first census. Missing management dates or dates before the first census gave \texttt{management = 0}. A date in or after the first census year gave \texttt{management = 1}. All plots were assigned \texttt{management\_cat = 1}, including those with missing dates. None was classified as primary forest. For retained plots with a dated intervention, \texttt{management\_since\_census1\_yrs} was the first-census year minus the intervention year, held constant thereafter. Missing management dates left this interval missing.

\subsubsection{RAINFOR Amazon Forest Inventory Network}
We obtained data of the RAINFOR Amazon Forest Inventory Network (\texttt{rainfor}) from published sources. Forest structural information (basal area, tree density), plot area, plot code, and census date were obtained from \citet{esquivelmuelbert25natplants}. Geographic coordinates were matched by complete plot code using \citet{bennett23natcc}, \citet{brienen_long-term_2015}, and the ForestPlots.net packages of \href{https://doi.org/10.5521/forestplots.net/2020_2}{Sullivan et al.\ (2020)}, \href{https://doi.org/10.5521/forestplots.net/2024_4}{Sullivan et al.\ (2025)}, and \href{https://doi.org/10.5521/forestplots.net/2020_6}{Esquivel-Muelbert et al.\ (2020)}. For each plot, we used complete coordinate pairs from the finest-resolution release available: $0.01^\circ$ in the Bennett and Sullivan packages, or $0.1^\circ$ in the Brienen and Esquivel-Muelbert packages. Differing duplicate pairs at the same finest resolution were averaged, with identical pairs counted only once. We excluded \texttt{NOU-59s}, for which no verified coordinate match was available. All remaining plots had exact plot-code matches. No coordinates were inferred from a shared plot-code prefix. Plot area was retained from the source summary table.

The source fields \texttt{number\_stems} and \texttt{total\_BA} supplied density and basal area per hectare, respectively, and therefore required no further division by \texttt{PlotArea}. QMD was calculated from their ratio using Eq.~\ref{eq:qmd2}. Decimal census dates were rounded to the nearest calendar year. The coordinate lookup retained source provenance and reported resolution.

Plots were assigned \texttt{management = 0} and \texttt{management\_cat = 2}. Forest plots of the RAINFOR network have been described as old-growth \citep{phillips09sci}, ``lacking signs of recent anthropogenic impacts'' \citep{brienen_long-term_2015}, and ``structurally intact'' \citep{bennett23natcc}.
